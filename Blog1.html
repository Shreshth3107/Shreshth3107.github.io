The Intersection of Engineering and Business

March 27, 2026 2 Min Read

As an engineering student, the focus is often narrowed down to the mechanics of creation. In technical courses like ECE 120, which I'm taking for my major, I focus on how to build systems from the ground up understanding the logic gates, the circuitry, and the fundamental physics that allow a machine to function.

"Understanding market viability, supply chain constraints, and the competitive landscape is what turns my projects into marketable products."

However, BADM 261 has shifted my perspective toward the "why." It isn't enough to simply build something that works; one must understand the ecosystem in which that product exists.

This blend between teachings from the Grainger College of Engineering and the Gies College of Business has led me to have a further appreciation for and understanding of innovation and product spaces. It represents a transition from being just a developer to becoming a product-minded innovator.

Moving forward, I aim to carry this dual perspective into every circuit I design and every line of code I write.
